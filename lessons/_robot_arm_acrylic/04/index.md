## Lesson Overview

In this lesson we will assemble the main robot arm.

## Instructions

### STEP 1. Attach Main Arm

Attach the main arm using:

- 2 x M3x12 Screws
- 2 x M3 Nuts (Regular)
- 1 x Acrylic Piece

<img src="arm4 (1).jpg" style="zoom:15%;" class="image center" />

Position the acrylic piece between the two sides of the main arm as shown. Insert the two M3x12 screws through the side plates and secure them with the M3 nuts.  Do not overtighten.

Make sure the acrylic piece is seated straight and the two sides of the arm remain parallel before tightening the screws.

### STEP 2. Attach Secondary Arm

Attach the secondary arm using:

- 3 x Standard Links
- 1 x Standard Link (with hole)
- 1 x Arm Joiner
- 1 x Long Link
- 1 x Cross Piece
- 3 x Circular Washers
- 5 x M3x6 Screws
- 2 x M3x10 Screws

> **IMPORTANT — FOLLOW STEPS 1–12 IN ORDER**
>
> Build the linkage in the exact sequence shown below. Pay close attention to which side of the arm each piece is attached to and the orientation of each acrylic piece.
>
> **Do not overtighten any screw that forms a moving joint.** The connected pieces should pivot freely by hand. If a joint is too tight, the servo motor may not be able to move the arm properly.

#### 2A. Build the first linkage

**Step 1:** Position the first **Standard Link** on the arm exactly as shown. Note the **Circular Washer** in between.  Check the orientation before installing the **M3x10** screw.

**Step 2:** Attach the **end hole** (see photo) of the **Long Link** at the upper pivot point using an **M3x6 Screw**. Leave the pivot just loose enough for the pieces to rotate freely.

Also attach the center hole of the **Long Link** using a **M3x10** screw.  Note the **Circular Washer** in between.

**Step 9:** Position the links so that your arm looks exactly like the photo. Make sure the **Long Link** and **Standard Link (with hole)** are aligned before continuing.

> **FINAL CHECK:** Compare your completed assembly with the final photo. Gently move the arm through its range of motion. All pivot joints should move freely without the acrylic pieces bending, rubbing, or binding.  Do not continue if a joint is difficult to move by hand. Check the orientation of the pieces and slightly loosen any pivot screw that is preventing free movement.
